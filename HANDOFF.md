- **2026-10-14 — Uber's session tokens have measured lifetimes, and we now see them coming.** Companies kept dropping at unpredictable hours and recovering only when a human clicked. Four outages on record, **every one** `cause=daemon` (the RAMEN stream refused 401/403 while the browser was fine), **every one** recovered `via=manual`, `relink_attempts=1`. ### Invariants — do NOT change these without explicit confirmation

These are settled product rules. Treat a request that seems to break one as a misunderstanding to clarify, not an instruction to execute.

- **Observe, don't control — with ONE carved-out write.** The only thing Reidey commands on Uber is a **dispatch-side decline of a still-pending offer** (`RejectOffers`, driver- or manager-triggered, gated by the per-company flag `tenants.settings['dispatch_reject']['enabled']`, absent = OFF). Never **accept** (`AcceptOffers` stays dormant — no route, no UI), never start/end a trip, never message a rider, never decide for the driver. Acceptance remains **inferred from Uber driver status only**. (See §1 and §2.7.)
- **Time model.** Timezone is **Europe/Berlin**; the **week starts Monday**; the **Uber fleet-day starts at 04:00**. All stats/windows use fleet-days.
- **Every offer push must carry** distance + dropoff + €/km with the €-quality badge. Geocode **before** notifying (`enrichForNotify`) so the push is never bare. A **multi-stop** ride pushes a new notification and shows per-stop addresses + per-leg km on both dashboard and app.
  - **Flag-gated exception (dispatch declines ON).** For a company with `dispatch_reject.enabled`, the **first** push is deliberately **bare** — `enrichForNotify` is skipped entirely, no geocoding at all — by the owner's explicit decision: a decline is only worth something while the offer is still open, and geocoding spends the window. The numbers arrive moments later over `OfferBroadcast` and the app fills the open pop-up in place. For every other tenant (the default) the invariant is byte-identical to before. Do not "restore" the geocode on the fast path.
- **Addresses.** Maximize geocoding; correct addresses from Uber waypoints after acceptance; **never change the city wrongly** — an authoritative city/PLZ must win over a mis-biased reverse-geocode.
- **i18n & numerals.** German is the **default** language (de/en/ar everywhere, RTL for ar). Money/distance/dates always render **Latin digits**, even in Arabic (`format.ts` / `latnLocale()`).
- **Scope discipline.** Keep bug fixes narrow; don't refactor working fundamentals to land a small fix.
- **Idempotency.** Offer ingest (on `offer_uuid`) and lifecycle transitions are idempotent by design — re-delivery from daemon/extension is expected and safe.

> **Sensitive/intentional:** a fixed **OTP test code** path exists (`services.otp_test_code` / `OTP_TEST_CODE`) so the team can log into the driver app without a live SMS — it is intentional; leave it unless the user says otherwise. Product posture is DSGVO "**detect, don't surveil**": don't add data collection beyond what a feature needs without flagging it.

## 1. Mental model

**What Reidey does.** German Uber-fleet operators run many drivers under one Uber "org". Uber sends ride **offers** to drivers with only a ~5-second accept window. Reidey taps the fleet's own **live Uber dispatch stream** (Uber's internal "RAMEN" server-sent-events feed), figures out which of the fleet's drivers each offer is for, and instantly pushes it to that driver's phone with the fare, €-quality, addresses and distance — so the driver can decide fast.

**The one principle: observe, don't control — with one carved-out write.** Reidey never accepts, starts, or ends a trip and never contacts a rider. It only *watches* Uber — except for a single, explicit, per-company-gated action: **declining a still-pending offer on the dispatch side** (§2.7). That one write exists because it is the only thing the fleet can do that the driver's own app cannot do for free: a decline issued from the **dispatch panel** does not count against the driver's Uber acceptance rate. Everything else is observation:

- **Offers** come in on the RAMEN stream (push).
- **Trip state** is *inferred*, never commanded. The daemon polls each driver's live Uber engagement status (~10s). When a driver goes idle → EN_ROUTE we infer "accepted"; → ON_TRIP is "started"; back to idle is "completed/canceled". The offer lifecycle is a read-only mirror of what the driver did inside the Uber app.

Keep this principle when extending the product. The dispatch decline (§2.7) is the **one** sanctioned write, and it is narrow on purpose: one mutation, one offer, still pending, a human in the loop every time, off unless a company's flag is on. Everything else that would *act on* Uber — **auto-accept** or any accept at all, cancelling a trip, messaging a rider, or deciding on the driver's behalf — remains out of scope and legally/ToS dangerous. The `AcceptOffers` mutation is shaped identically to `RejectOffers` and is therefore trivially reachable: it is left **dormant deliberately** (no route, no UI, nothing that creates an accept command). Do not wire it. And note the escalation honestly: writing to Uber is a bigger ToS exposure than reading it (`docs/13-deployment.md`).

**Why the odd plumbing.** Uber blocks datacenter IPs (RAMEN 404s, roster returns 0). So the stream is held either server-side through a **residential proxy** (the `dispatch-daemon`), or in the **manager's own browser** via a Chrome extension (real residential IP). Both are just *capture* transports; the Laravel backend is the brain and single source of truth.

**Naming note.** The product is **Reidey** (domain `reidey.de`). You will see legacy names in code/comments/docs — `Ridy`, `DASHCAM`, `fleeteye`. They refer to the same system across its rename history.

## 2. Architecture deep-dive

### 2.1 Offer pipeline (capture → ingest → match → geocode → push)

**Transport in.** The daemon posts raw offer batches to:

```
POST /api/v1/internal/dispatch/ingest      header: X-Dispatch-Secret: <DISPATCH_INGEST_SECRET>
```

Guarded by the `dispatch.secret` middleware. Body: `{ offers: [...], seq?: int }`. There is a *second*, user-session-authenticated path used by the browser extension: `POST /api/v1/dispatch/offers/ingest` (`DispatchOfferController@ingest`).

**Key files/classes:**

| Concern | File / class |
| --- | --- |
| Internal ingest endpoint | `backend/app/Http/Controllers/Api/V1/DispatchIngestController.php` |
| Secret middleware | `backend/app/Http/Middleware/VerifyDispatchSecret.php` (alias `dispatch.secret`, `hash_equals` on `X-Dispatch-Secret` vs `services.dispatch.ingest_secret`) |
| Core ingestor | `backend/app/Domain/Dispatch/DispatchOfferIngestor.php` |
| Geocoding | `backend/app/Domain/Dispatch/TripGeocoder.php` |
| Driver push builder | `backend/app/Domain/Notifications/DispatchNotifier.php` |

**Flow (`DispatchOfferIngestor::ingest($tenantId, $offer, $seq)`):**

1. The daemon never knows internal tenant IDs — the controller resolves the tenant by `Tenant::where('uber_org_uuid', $offer['partnerUUID'])`. No match → `no_tenant`.
2. Idempotent on `offer_uuid` (unique `(tenant_id, offer_uuid)` index; also catches the `QueryException` race) → `duplicate`; empty uuid → `skipped_no_uuid`.
3. Driver match: `Driver::where('uber_driver_uuid', $offer['driverInfo']['driverUUID'])`. Stored with `status = OfferStatus::Pending`. Fare parsed from `formattedUFP` via `DriverStatsService::parseFare`.
4. If a driver is linked: `TripGeocoder::enrich($record)` then `DispatchNotifier::notify($record)`, each in its own try/catch — a geocode or push failure never loses the offer. Result is `routed` (driver linked) or `unlinked_driver`.

**Geocoding (`TripGeocoder`).** Endpoints come from **config**, not constants: `config('services.geo.nominatim_url')` + `/search` (and `/reverse`) and `config('services.geo.osrm_url')` + `/route/v1/driving` — so prod points at the self-hosted stack (see `docs/self-hosted-geo.md`). `MAX_ATTEMPTS = 20`, 6s HTTP timeout, custom `UA`. Geocodes pickup + dropoff (results cached in `geocode_cache` keyed by query; transient 429/5xx/timeout are *not* cached so they retry). Both ends resolved → OSRM route → `distance_m` + `route_geometry` (GeoJSON) + stamps `geo_synced_at`. OSRM down → haversine straight-line distance, no geometry. The `offers:backfill-geo` scheduled command retries offers whose lazy enrich failed; `--reset` re-queues offers that gave up **and** clears the cached *misses* for their addresses (a permanently-cached miss used to defeat it). A cached miss now expires after `MISS_TTL_DAYS = 30`, and `geocode-cache:prune` (nightly) drops expired misses + long-unused hits.

**Push (`DispatchNotifier`).** Deliberately *data-only / language-neutral* (the phone localizes), except the multi-stop alert, which is worded + localized on purpose. Sends to every `DeviceToken` of the linked driver via the injected `PushSender` — **in parallel** when the transport implements `SendsPushInBulk` (`FcmPushSender` pools the requests), inside a wall-clock budget so the push never outlives the accept window.
- **Title** (`buildTitle`): `"5.85 €€ · 12.3 km · €1.26/km | Peter"` — `buildNumbers()` (fare + €-quality + distance + €/km) then `" | " + rider_first_name`.
- **€-quality** (`euroSigns`) by per-km rate `fare/km`: `>= 3 → €€€`, `> 1 → €€`, else `€`. Unknown fare/km → single `€`.
- **Body** (`buildBody`): `pickup \n dropoff`, Latinized (`AddressLatinizer`) and country-stripped. An approximate distance (geo_confidence ≠ `exact`) renders with a `~` prefix.
- **Data payload** — one builder, `offerData()`, shared by the offer and multi-stop pushes: `categoryId, offer_id, offer_uuid, pickup, dropoff, pickup_lat/lng, dropoff_lat/lng, geo_source, fare, fare_amount, distance_m, accept_window, received_at, stops` (+ `stops_count` on the multi-stop alert).
- **Owners.** A manager running the app in owner mode gets their own copy — driver name on the body's first line — from the **queued** `NotifyOwnersOfOffer` (owners have no 5-second window), for the multi-stop follow-up too. `notify()` therefore returns the count of the *driver's* devices.

### 2.2 Offer lifecycle (inference + guarded transitions)

**Enum** `backend/app/Domain/Dispatch/OfferStatus.php`: `Pending, Accepted, Started, Completed, Rejected, Canceled`.

**Guarded transitions** (`allowedNext()`):

```
Pending   → Accepted | Rejected
Accepted  → Started  | Canceled
Started   → Completed
Rejected  → Accepted            (late-detected accept overturns a recent rejection, within the 3-min grace)
Completed → (terminal)
Canceled  → (terminal)
```

`OfferLifecycle` (`backend/app/Domain/Dispatch/OfferLifecycle.php`) runs every transition inside a DB transaction with `lockForUpdate()`; a disallowed transition is a **no-op** (idempotent). Constants:
- `ATTRIBUTION_MINUTES = 15` — a stale offer older than this isn't attributed to a new engagement.
- `ACCEPTED_STALE_MINUTES = 20` — ACCEPTED-but-never-started is force-canceled after this.
- `MAX_TRIP_MINUTES = 100` — a STARTED offer open longer is force-completed (the over-long-trip safety net).
- `LATE_ACCEPT_GRACE_MINUTES = 3` — how recently a REJECTED offer must have been rejected to still overturn to Accepted (a late-detected take); past this it stays rejected.

**Inference** (`backend/app/Domain/Fleet/DriverStatusIngestor.php`). Engagement `level()`: string contains `ON_TRIP` → 2, `EN_ROUTE` → 1, else 0 (idle). Edge rules (`applyTransition`):
- idle → engaged: find the pending offer → `accept()`. If it jumped straight to ON_TRIP, also `start()`.
- EN_ROUTE → ON_TRIP: active offer → `start()`.
- ON_TRIP → EN_ROUTE (back-to-back): `complete()` the started offer, then `accept()` the next takeable offer newer than it.
- engaged → idle (genuine idle-ONLINE return): `complete()` the active offer if Started, else `cancel()` if only Accepted; AND `rejectPendingFor()` any still-pending offer the driver never took (available again → passed on it).

Uber's 0,0 coords are nulled; malformed rows are per-row try/catch so one bad row never fails a batch. After each batch it runs `expirePending()` and `finalizeStale()`. `expirePending` resolves a pending offer by the driver's availability: **HOLD while ENGAGED** (a possible back-to-back, until the 2h cap); **REJECT when IDLE-online and the accept window (+30s) has elapsed** (free and didn't take it); **REJECT when OFFLINE**; **REJECT past the 2h cap**. An engaged driver is never timed out (so a back-to-back never flickers rejected). A pending offer is also resolved by events: a newer offer supersedes it, or the driver engages and it's accepted.

**Safety nets (scheduled).** `offers:finalize-stale` (`App\Console\Commands\FinalizeStaleOffers`, every 5 min) force-completes STARTED offers older than 100 min and force-cancels ACCEPTED older than 20 min, across all tenants (covers idle daemons). `offers:expire-pending` (every minute) is the idle-tenant backstop that rejects a pending offer whose driver has since gone offline (or past the 2h cap).

### 2.3 Driver-app auth (two guards)

Sanctum with **two guards** (`backend/config/auth.php`): `web` (session/`users`, the SPA dashboard) and `driver` (**sanctum bearer** / `drivers` provider → `App\Domain\Fleet\Models\Driver`, which has `HasApiTokens`).

**Invite → activate flow** (`backend/app/Domain/Fleet/DriverInvitationService.php`, `INVITE_TTL_DAYS = 7`):
1. Manager invites: `POST /api/v1/drivers/{driver}/invite` (`DriverInviteController@send`). Recipient = `driver.email`, falling back to `driver.uber_email`. Sets `invite_token = Str::random(48)`, `invited_at`, emails template `driver_invite` with a link `{frontend_url}/driver/activate?token=…`.
2. Driver activates within 7 days: `POST /api/v1/driver/activate` sets the password, stamps `activated_at`, clears the token, and `createToken('driver-app')` issues the Sanctum bearer.
3. Driver-app auth controller: `backend/app/Http/Controllers/Api/V1/Driver/DriverAuthController.php` (`invite` preview, `activate`, `login`, `me`, `update`, `logout`).

**Fleet-owner mode.** A manager signing into the driver app gets a token minted `('driver-app-owner', ['fleet:read'])` — deliberately narrower than a dashboard session, because it is obtained with an emailed 6-digit code and no password. `EnsureDashboardToken` confines any token holding only that ability to `api/v1/driver/fleet/*` (the same mechanism that confines the extension's `fleet-session:write` token), so it can never reach the manager API.

**Subscription gate.** `backend/app/Http/Middleware/EnsureDriverTenantActive.php` (alias `driver.active`) reads `user->tenant->stateReason()`; if non-null it returns `403 { message: 'account_suspended', reason, support_email, support_whatsapp }`. Applied to the driver's data routes (`me`, `devices`, `home`, `stats`, `offers`), so a driver of a lapsed company is blocked even with a valid token. `DriverAuthController::guardSuspended()` also blocks login/activate.

### 2.4 Notifications (bell + web push + email)

`backend/app/Domain/Notifications/Notifier.php` is the **single place** dashboard notifications are created. For each recipient it:
1. Writes a typed `AppNotification` (database channel — stores `{type, params, href}`, *not* rendered text, so the frontend localizes).

### 2.7 Dispatch-side offer decline (the one write to Uber)

**What it is.** The driver's app (or a manager on the dashboard) can **decline a still-pending offer on the fleet's dispatch side**. Uber's own panel does this with a `RejectOffers` GraphQL mutation on `vsdispatch.uber.com` — the *same* host and the *same* captured cookies the daemon already holds for the RAMEN offer stream, so no new session, no new credential, no Arkose challenge. The point is not speed for its own sake: a decline issued from the **panel** does not count against the driver's Uber acceptance rate, which a decline in the driver's own Uber app does.

**It is OFF by default.** Everything below is gated on `Tenant::dispatchRejectEnabled()`, reading `tenants.settings['dispatch_reject']['enabled']` — **absent = OFF**. The operator flips it per company from the **admin companies page** — the switch beside El-Professor, `PATCH /api/v1/admin/companies/{tenant}/dispatch-reject`, super-admin only, confirmed on the way ON and one click on the way OFF. The flag lives in settings JSON and **not** in env on purpose (see Gotchas: CI rewrites the prod `.env` from GitHub secrets on every deploy, so an env flag would silently reset). The same flag also turns on the bare instant push below.

**The path.** Nothing in a request ever talks to Uber — the backend only writes a **row**, and the daemon (which holds the residential-IP session) performs the mutation:

```
offer lands (RAMEN -> DispatchOfferIngestor)
  -> bare instant push + OfferBroadcast('new') with inline fields   [flag ON]
  -> driver taps Reject / manager clicks Decline
      POST /api/v1/driver/offers/{offer}/dispatch-reject      (auth:driver)
      POST /api/v1/dispatch/offers/{offer}/dispatch-reject    (can:offers.manage)
  -> DispatchActionService::requestReject()  -> offer_dispatch_commands row (status=queued)
  -> daemon long-poll  GET  /api/v1/internal/dispatch/commands?wait=N   -> claims it (status=claimed, attempts++)
  -> daemon POSTs RejectOffers to vsdispatch.uber.com with that session's cookies
  -> daemon  POST /api/v1/internal/dispatch/commands/{command}/result  {is_success, error, latency_ms}
  -> DispatchActionService::applyResult()
        success -> stamp dispatch_offers.dispatch_rejected_at + OfferLifecycle::reject()
        failure -> log, store uber_error, offer stays PENDING
```

| Concern | File / class |
| --- | --- |
| The service (queue a command, decide if it's allowed, apply the verdict) | `backend/app/Domain/Dispatch/DispatchActionService.php` |
| Claiming + the long poll + the expiry sweep | `backend/app/Domain/Dispatch/DispatchCommandQueue.php` |
| Command row | `Domain\Dispatch\Models\OfferDispatchCommand` (+ enums `DispatchCommandStatus`, `DispatchCommandAction`) |
| Daemon-facing endpoints | `DispatchDaemonController::commands` / `::commandResult` (`DispatchCommandResultRequest`) |
| Client-facing endpoints | `Driver/DriverOfferController::dispatchReject`, `DispatchOfferController::dispatchReject` |
| Expiry schedule | `App\Console\Commands\ExpireDispatchCommands` (`dispatch:expire-commands`, every minute) |
| Daemon executor | `dispatch-daemon/src/commands.js` (the loop) + `src/stream.js` (`DISPATCH_MUTATIONS`) — **incomplete, see below** |

**`requestReject()` returns a verdict, not an exception.** It answers `['status' => 'queued'|'duplicate'|'already_declined'|'refused', 'reason'?, 'command_id'?]`, and both HTTP routes return that array **verbatim** under `data` with **200**. A refusal is not an error — the client shows the reason, localized. `404` only when the offer is not the caller's. The seven refusal reasons are `disabled`, `not_pending`, `missing_real_offer_uuid`, `missing_driver_uuid`, `window_closed`, `no_session`, `too_many_attempts` (`MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3`). `canReject()` runs the same predicates with no side effects and is what feeds `can_decline` on `DispatchOfferResource` — the UI never recomputes the verdict client-side. (`canReject()` reads `$offer->tenant`, so every offer list eager-loads `tenant`; dropping that load reintroduces an N+1 across the whole feed.)

**Idempotency** is the unique index `offer_dispatch_commands (dispatch_offer_id, action)`: a double tap, a re-sent notification action, two managers clicking at once — all collapse to one row and a `duplicate`. A re-delivered *result* for an already-`succeeded` command short-circuits. The claim is deliberately **one guarded UPDATE per row** (`where status = queued` -> `claimed`), not one batch UPDATE, because a batch UPDATE reports *how many* rows it changed and never *which* — it cannot tell a winning shard from a losing one.

**The long poll.** `GET internal/dispatch/commands?wait=<seconds>` parks the request (up to `DispatchCommandQueue::MAX_WAIT_SECONDS = 25`, polling every 150 ms, `CLAIM_LIMIT = 5` per return) until this shard has a claimable command, then returns — an **empty array** on timeout. It heartbeats like `sessions` but deliberately **skips** `reconcileAssignments()`: a daemon box must not take over streams merely by asking for work. A result POSTed for a command belonging to another shard is a **409**. Because a parked request **occupies an FPM worker** for its whole life, this one path has its own pool — `[command]` on `:9002` in `docker/php/php-fpm.d/zz-pools.conf`, matched ahead of the generic internal handler in `docker/Caddyfile`. That split is the *inverse* of the `[dispatch]` one: `[dispatch]` exists to protect ingest capacity, and parks would eat it.

**Deadlines are measured, not assumed.** `DELIVERY_GRACE_SECONDS = 60` and `MIN_WINDOW_SECONDS = 15` give `deliveryDeadline()` (counted from `offer_generated_at` when Uber sent it, else `received_at`), after which the command is `expired` by the scheduled sweep. These numbers are **deliberately generous**: across **1003 production offers** `acceptWindowInSeconds` was exactly **5** every single time, but nobody has measured whether 5 s is the *driver's* window or the *panel's*. So the design attempts the late decline and records what Uber answers — `latency_ms` and `uber_error` on every command row are the dataset that will settle it. Until it is settled, **never write a comment, string or doc claiming the 5 s window is beatable**, and do not tighten these constants on a guess.

**Frozen payload.** The command row copies `offer_uuid`, `real_offer_uuid`, `offer_type` and `driver_uuid` at queue time, so the daemon needs no lookup and a later edit to the offer cannot change what was sent. `offer_type` is **`'JOB'`, a client constant** (`DispatchActionService::DEFAULT_OFFER_TYPE`): the key is absent from all 1003 captured payloads and no alternative key carries a type, yet Uber's own panel sends `"JOB"`. `realOfferUUID` was present on all 1003 — a missing one is a refusal, never a guess.

**The bare instant push (same flag).** With the flag ON, `DispatchOfferIngestor::notifyDriver()` **skips `enrichForNotify` entirely** (skipped, not shortened) and `DispatchNotifier::notify($offer, fast: true)` inverts the order to **Reverb first, FCM second** under a hard 0.3 s cap, adding `data['fast'] = '1'`. `OfferBroadcast::broadcastWith()` for `reason === 'new'` carries the free fields inline — `offer_uuid, fare, pickup, dropoff, rider_name, accept_window, offer_generated_at, received_at` — so the app's pop-up renders with **zero extra fetch** and enriches in place when the geocoded detail lands. `NotifyOwnersOfOffer` returns early once `dispatch_rejected_at` is set. Flag OFF -> the old path, unchanged.

**Acceptance-rate arithmetic.** The owner's rule: an offer declined on the panel **never reached the driver**, so it is not an offer of theirs at all. All four surfaces exclude it from both total and accepted and report it separately as `dispatch_declined` — `DriverStatsService::forDriver`, `Driver/DriverDashboardController::summary`, `Driver/FleetController::summary`, and `DispatchOfferController::stats` (a raw conditional aggregate). `DispatchOffer::scopeReachedDriver()` is the denominator; use it rather than re-deriving the condition.

**`AcceptOffers` is dormant on purpose.** It is the same mutation shape with a different `operationName`, so `DispatchCommandAction` names it and the daemon carries the document for executor symmetry — but **nothing creates an accept command**: no route, no UI, no caller. Leave it that way (see §1 and the Invariants).

**The daemon executor.** `RamenStream.dispatchAction({action, offerUUID, realOfferUUID, offerType, driverUUID})` is the only code in the product that writes to Uber. It runs on the stream that is already receiving that company's offers, because the mutation needs the same vsdispatch jar (`headers()` — **not** `supplierHeaders()`, which is the fleethub jar) and the same residential exit; Uber answers RAMEN from a datacenter IP with 404, and the backend's stored jar can lag a rotation by a whole session poll.

Three things about it are load-bearing and easy to undo by accident:

- **It never throws.** Every outcome is a resolved result, because the caller reports whatever comes back and a throw would leave the driver's command hanging until it expires.
- **`isSuccess: null` is not `false`.** A timeout, a 5xx or an unreadable body means *we got no verdict* — Uber may well have applied the decline — so the backend must not record a confirmed failure. Only a real `false` from `offerResponse` is a refusal. The tri-state survives the whole way: `api.js` sends `is_success` as `true | false | null`, the FormRequest validates it `present|nullable|boolean`, and `uber_success` stays NULL for "no verdict" so the measurement can tell the two apart. Collapsing it to a boolean anywhere re-introduces the bug.
- **`accept` is refused by the executor itself.** `dispatchAction` returns early for any action other than `reject`, even though the `AcceptOffers` document sits right there in `DISPATCH_MUTATIONS`. `action` arrives from the backend, so this is the last line of defence if a bug or a hand-edited row ever produces one. Acceptance stays INFERRED.
- **`latency_ms` is the OFFER'S AGE when Uber answered, computed on the backend** (`DispatchActionService::endToEndLatency`) from `offer_generated_at` — not the daemon's own call duration, which it reports separately and which the backend logs as `call_ms`. This matters because the age is the only figure that can reveal Uber's unknown dispatch-decline deadline; measuring our request duration instead would answer a question nobody asked. The clock is sanity-bounded (0 to 10 minutes) so an epoch disagreement cannot poison the column.
- **A 401/403 is deliberately NOT routed into `handleStreamAuthFailure`.** That handler flags the session `needs_relink` and stops the stream, so letting a refused decline reach it would let one tap take down a company's entire offer ingest. A write may be gated differently from the read stream (CSRF, a challenge, an org permission the manager simply lacks), so a 403 here is no evidence the offer session is dead — and `/ack` + `/recv` have their own auth handling that will say so within seconds if it really is. This is the same lesson `handleSupplierAuthFailure` records from the 4.5-hour outage on 2026-09-12.

`src/commands.js` holds the claim/execute/report loop, started from `main()` **after** the first `reconcileTick()` (so the streams a command needs already exist) and stopped **before** the streams in `shutdown()` (so a parked long-poll cannot hold the shutdown past Docker's grace period). The four env vars are in `.env.example`. `test/dispatch-action.test.js` covers the mutation body, the jar choice, refusal-vs-no-verdict, a network failure, an incomplete payload and the error clipping, driven through undici's `MockAgent` as the stream's dispatcher — stubbing a global `fetch` would not work, since `stream.js` imports fetch from undici directly.

## 4. Conventions

**Service-layer architecture.** Thin controllers → services (`app/Domain/**`) → models/repositories. Business logic lives in the domain services (`DispatchOfferIngestor`, `OfferLifecycle`, `DriverStatusIngestor`, `Notifier`, `DriverInvitationService`, `ProxyPool`, `SystemHealthService`, …), not controllers. Dependencies are injected (e.g. `PushSender` is an interface bound in `AppServiceProvider`).

**i18n.** Three languages everywhere: **de / en / ar**, and **German is the default**.
- Frontend keys live in `frontend/src/lib/i18n/` — chrome/nav in `dictionaries.ts`, per-screen strings split across `screens-a.ts`, `screens-b.ts`, `screens-ridy.ts`, `screens-admin.ts` (merged under `screens.*`). Add a key to **all three** locales.
- Driver app: `driver-app/src/lib/i18n.ts` (flat dot-key dictionaries).
- **RTL** for Arabic: `RTL_LOCALES = ["ar"]`, sets `dir="rtl"` + `lang` on `<html>`.
- **Money = Latin digits always, one fixed shape.** Even in Arabic, prices/distances/dates render Western numerals. Frontend uses `latnLocale()` → `"ar-u-nu-latn"` and `toLatinDigits()` (`frontend/src/lib/utils.ts`). The driver app (`driver-app/src/lib/format.ts`) formats **money + distance in `de-DE`** — one canonical German shape everywhere: fare `"4,10 €"`, rate `"1,77 €/km"`, distance `"2,3 km"` (comma decimal, € **after** the amount, Latin digits) — and time in `en-DE` (`"02/09, 11:12"`). It formats from the **numeric** amount and never Uber's pre-formatted string (which mixed `"EUR"`/`"€"` and flipped the symbol side — the old inconsistency).

**Theme tokens.** Tailwind v4 CSS-first, no `tailwind.config.js`. Semantic tokens are CSS vars in `frontend/src/app/globals.css` on `:root` (light) / `.dark` (dark), exposed via `@theme inline` (`bg-surface`, `text-ink`, `border-line`, status tones `success/danger/warning/info/accent`). Dark mode is class-based (`@custom-variant dark`). Never hard-code hex — use the token utilities. The driver app mirrors this via a `useColors()` hook (`driver-app/src/lib/theme.ts`) returning a light/dark palette, including offer-status hues.

**Testing patterns.** Feature tests under `backend/tests`, run on sqlite `:memory:`. For notifications/mail use `Mail::fake()` or the array transport; for push, bind a spy/fake `PushSender` (the interface makes this clean — `LogPushSender` is the no-op default when FCM creds are absent). Run `vendor/bin/pint --test` for style and `php artisan test`. Frontend gate is `npm run build`; lint is advisory (React 19 hook rules flag legacy patterns).

| `COMMAND_POLL_WAIT_MS` / `COMMAND_API_TIMEOUT_MS` | daemon | how long the daemon asks the backend to park its command long-poll (20 s, clamped to a 60 s ceiling) and the HTTP deadline for that call (defaults to the wait **+5 s**). The timeout **must** exceed the wait, or every park aborts exactly while the backend is doing what it was asked — `config.js` validates this at boot. |
| `DISPATCH_ACTION_TIMEOUT_MS` | daemon | deadline for the `RejectOffers` POST to Uber (2500 ms, floor 500). |
| `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL` | frontend build | API base for the browser. |

> Per-company proxies are stored in the DB (`proxies.url`, encrypted), not env; `UBER_PROXY_URL` is only the global fallback.

> The four `COMMAND*` / `DISPATCH_ACTION_*` vars above are read by `dispatch-daemon/src/config.js` but are **not yet written into `.env.example`**, and the command loop is **not started** — see §2.7 for what is still missing in the daemon.

## 6. Deployment (prod: Docker Compose + Caddy on one VPS)

Prod stack `docker-compose.prod.yml` (domain `reidey.de`). Services:

| Service | What |
| --- | --- |
| `caddy` | TLS edge + reverse proxy (auto Let's Encrypt). Given a network alias of `${DOMAIN}` so the daemon reaches the API by public domain over the internal network (VPS hairpin fix). |
| `mysql` | MySQL 8, volume `dbdata`, healthcheck. |
| `backend` | Laravel PHP-FPM. **`./backend` is volume-mounted** (`:/var/www` + anonymous `/var/www/vendor`) — so a deploy is a code sync, not an image rebuild. |
| `scheduler` | same image, `command: php artisan schedule:work` — runs Laravel's cron in one long process (no host crontab). |
| `queue` | same image, `command: php artisan queue:work --queue=default --tries=3 --backoff=10 --max-time=3600` — the primary queue worker (the scheduler's per-minute `queue:work --stop-when-empty` is a backstop). |
| `reverb` | Laravel Reverb WebSocket server (broadcasting) — the driver app AND the manager dashboard subscribe for live offer + driver-map updates (isolated `company.{tenantId}` channel); the poll remains a fallback. |
| `frontend` | Next.js, built with `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL=https://${DOMAIN}`. |
| `dispatch-daemon` | Node RAMEN daemon; env `RIDY_API_URL=https://${DOMAIN}`, `DISPATCH_INGEST_SECRET`, `UBER_*`. |
| `nominatim`, `osrm` | optional self-hosted geocoding/routing (behind the `geo` profile — see `docs/self-hosted-geo.md`); `TripGeocoder` falls back to the public OSM services when unset. |

Backend, scheduler and queue share env via the `x-app-env` YAML anchor (prod uses **database** queue/cache; no Redis/Horizon — those are dev-only in `docker-compose.yml`).

**First-time provision** (see `docs/13-deployment.md`): DNS A record → install Docker → clone → `cp .env.prod.example .env` and fill `DOMAIN`, DB passwords, `DISPATCH_INGEST_SECRET` → generate `APP_KEY` → then:

```bash
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml up -d --build
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml exec backend php artisan migrate --force --seed
# seed creates manager@fleet.de / password — change it immediately.
```

**Routine deploy** (backend is mounted, so no image rebuild for backend/scheduler):

```bash
git pull
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml exec backend php artisan migrate --force
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml exec backend php artisan config:clear
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml restart backend scheduler queue
```

> **The dispatch-decline feature needs more than a code sync.** `docker/php/php-fpm.d/zz-pools.conf` is `COPY`-ed **into the backend image** (`docker/backend.Dockerfile`), so the new `[command]` pool on `:9002` only exists after a **rebuild** of the services on that image; and `docker/Caddyfile` is a read-only bind mount, so its new `/api/v1/internal/dispatch/commands*` matcher needs Caddy **recreated/restarted**. A manual routine deploy (above) does neither — without them the long poll falls back to the shared pool and parked requests eat the ingest workers that `[dispatch]` exists to protect. CI already handles it (`BUILT_SERVICES` includes `backend scheduler reverb queue`, and `APP_SERVICES` is force-recreated including `caddy`). By hand:
>
> ```bash
> docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml up -d --build --force-recreate --renew-anon-volumes backend scheduler queue reverb
> docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml up -d --force-recreate caddy
> ```

**Frontend / daemon changes** (built into images) need a rebuild:

```bash
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml up -d --build frontend
docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml up -d --build dispatch-daemon
```

> The CI deploy workflow (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`) automates an SSH deploy on push to `main`: `git reset --hard origin/main`, writes `.env` line by line from the repo secrets (`env_line` quotes + escapes each value — an unquoted heredoc used to let the shell expand a `$` or backtick INSIDE a secret), `up -d --build`, waits for MySQL with `mysqladmin ping`, then runs `migrate --force` **once, unredirected — a failing migration now fails the job** (it used to be retried 30× with its error discarded and still report success), then `config:cache` (note: `config:cache`, not `config:clear` — clear it manually if you're debugging config) and finally a `curl` of `/api/v1/health`, so "Deploy complete" means the site actually answers.

**FCM in prod.** Already automated: the deploy writes the `FCM_CREDENTIALS_JSON` GitHub secret to `backend/storage/app/fcm-credentials.json` and points `FCM_CREDENTIALS` at `/var/www/storage/app/fcm-credentials.json` in the generated `.env` (`FCM_PROJECT_ID` optional — it falls back to the JSON's `project_id`). Rotating the service account = update the secret and redeploy. If push is only logged, the secret is empty: `AppServiceProvider` binds `FcmPushSender` only when the credentials **file exists**, else `LogPushSender`. Web push uses the same project and is configured in the frontend bundle.

**Uber linking on prod.** Automated server login is blocked (datacenter IP). The manager installs the Chrome extension, logs into Uber in their own browser, the session is captured (`POST /api/v1/fleet-session`), and the daemon picks it up automatically.

## 8. Runbook (diagnosing common issues)

**Offers not arriving at all.**
- Check the daemon logs (`docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml logs -f dispatch-daemon`). Look for `starting stream …`, `stopping stream …`, `session poll failed`.
- Is there an active fleet session? `GET /api/v1/internal/dispatch/sessions` (with the secret) should list it. If the manager never linked, or the session flipped to `needs_relink` (Uber returned 401/403), no stream runs — the manager must re-link via the extension.
- **Proxy**: a company with no proxy (and no global `UBER_PROXY_URL`) connects directly → Uber blocks it (RAMEN 404, roster 0). Assign a proxy in the admin panel; the daemon restarts that stream automatically.
- **After a re-link**: the supervisor now fingerprints cookies by value (`jarFingerprint`), so a reconnect restarts the stream on its own — a manual daemon restart should no longer be needed. If a re-link still doesn't take effect, check the daemon logs for `stopping stream … (cookies changed)` then `starting stream …`.
- Verify `DISPATCH_INGEST_SECRET` matches on both sides (mismatch → 401 on `/ingest`).

**Our data disagrees with Uber (offer stuck / wrong lifecycle state).**
- This is inference, so it's the `DriverStatusIngestor` / `OfferLifecycle` or a status-capture gap. Check the ~10s status poll is running (daemon logs) and reaching `POST /sessions/{id}/statuses`.
- Stuck STARTED/ACCEPTED offers are cleaned by `offers:finalize-stale` (5-min schedule; 100-min / 20-min thresholds). Confirm the `scheduler` container is up (`schedule:work`).
- A missed accept-window rejection is cleaned by `offers:expire-pending` (every minute).

**Push not received (driver app).**
- First confirm the sender: FCM is wired on prod (§6), so a *logged* push means the credentials file is missing — check `FCM_CREDENTIALS` in the container's `.env`, that `storage/app/fcm-credentials.json` exists, and that the `FCM_CREDENTIALS_JSON` secret isn't empty. Grep backend logs for the logged push.
- Otherwise: is a `device_token` registered for that driver? Is Android notification permission granted? Is it a **dev/production build** (Expo Go can't do FCM)?
- Geocode/enrich failures don't block push (offer still routes); they only mean the body lacks distance — check `offers:backfill-geo` and Nominatim/OSRM rate limits.

**How long was a company without offers?** The super-admin company page → Session shows every outage in the last 30 days: when it started, how long it lasted, and whether it was fixed by the extension's self-heal (`auto`), a manager Connect (`manual`) or an Uber page load (`page`). The raw rows are in `fleet_session_outages`.

**A session jar fingerprint is read from three places and they must agree.** The stream constructor stores it, `liveJarFingerprint()` recomputes it from the live Maps, and the supervisor's `reconcile()` recomputes it from the backend row — then `persistJar()` and `reconcile()` COMPARE those readings to decide whether anything rotated and whether to restart the stream. All three now go through **one** function, `sessionJarFingerprint()`, which applies the supplier fallback (`effectiveSupplierCookies()`): a row with no `supplier_cookies` runs on the RAMEN jar, so its fingerprint must be computed that way too. They used not to — the constructor hashed the raw, empty column while `supplierJar` had already fallen back — so for every legacy session row the stored and live fingerprints could never be equal, `persistJar()`'s "nothing changed" guard never fired, the daemon wrote the jar back on the first `Set-Cookie` even though nothing had rotated, and `reconcile()` read the drift as "cookies changed" and restarted the stream — the cookie-churn pattern behind the offer-loss incident. Fixed 2026-10-11 with `test/jar-fingerprint.test.js`, which pins all three readings together (3 of its 8 cases fail against the old code). **If you ever add a fourth place that needs this fingerprint, call that function — do not inline the hash.**

**A company's offers stopped (session flips to `needs_relink`).** A Fleet Hub 401/403 no longer tears down the offer stream (2026-09-13). To see WHY a session dropped, read the network feed for that tenant — `kind='session'` rows carry `payload.source` (`daemon`/`extension`/`admin`) and `event` (`needs_relink` vs `supplier_degraded`). A `supplier_degraded` row means offers KEPT flowing (only roster/status degraded); a `needs_relink` means the offer stream itself was rejected (`daemon` = daemon ack/recv, `extension` = the manager's browser with no live daemon stream, `admin` = a forced relink). If a session keeps breaking right after each reconnect, suspect the extension's "sign out other Uber sessions" step evicting the current session — confirm the devices-page button says "**other**", not "all devices".

**A driver tapped Reject and nothing happened.** Walk the row, not the code:

1. Is the company's flag on? `Tenant::dispatchRejectEnabled()` — `settings['dispatch_reject']['enabled']`. OFF means the endpoint answers `200 {status: refused, reason: disabled}` and the app shows that reason; that is working as designed.
2. Was a command written? `select * from offer_dispatch_commands where dispatch_offer_id = ?`. No row → read the `reason` the endpoint returned (seven possibilities, §2.7). A row with `status = queued` and no `claimed_at` → **no daemon claimed it**, which today is the expected state: the daemon executor is unfinished (§2.7), so every command expires. A row `claimed` with no `completed_at` → the daemon took it and never reported; it will be retried up to `MAX_ATTEMPTS = 3` while the deadline holds.
3. `status = failed` → read `uber_error` and `latency_ms`. The offer **stays pending** on purpose: we never mark an offer declined that Uber did not confirm. A high `latency_ms` on a failure is the measurement this feature exists to collect — don't delete the row.
4. `status = succeeded` but the UI still shows it open → check `dispatch_offers.dispatch_rejected_at` and the `OfferBroadcast`; the dashboard confirms off the column, not off the POST response (it flips to "unconfirmed" after a 12 s grace rather than claim a decline that did not happen).
5. Long poll suspiciously slow or ingest suddenly sluggish → confirm the `[command]` FPM pool and the Caddy matcher actually shipped (§6). Without them the parks run in the shared pool.

**Where to look:** daemon → container logs; backend events → `RidyLog::event` entries and `storage/logs`; Uber session/daemon/proxy health at a glance → the super-admin **System Health** page (`GET /api/v1/admin/system-health`).

## 9. Gotchas

- **Next.js 16 ≠ your training data.** `frontend/AGENTS.md` warns that APIs/conventions differ; it says to read `node_modules/next/dist/docs/` before writing frontend code. Those docs only exist after `npm install` (they're not committed). Frontend is pinned to `next@16.2.7`, React 19.
- **The tenant global scope is silent.** Queries on `BelongsToTenant` models are auto-filtered to the active tenant — and the scope is a **no-op** when no tenant is set (console, seeders, the daemon controller). In those contexts you must scope explicitly (`where('tenant_id', …)` or `withoutGlobalScopes()`), as the driver-invite lookup does.
- **Two auth guards.** Dashboard = `auth:sanctum` (SPA cookie, `web`/`users`); driver app = `auth:driver` (bearer, `drivers`). Don't cross them. Driver data routes also need `driver.active`.
- **Geocoding rate limits.** Nominatim/OSRM are the public shared instances (1 req/s etiquette). Enrichment is best-effort, cached, capped at 5 attempts, and backfilled on a schedule — don't add per-request geocoding to hot paths.
- **Idempotency everywhere.** Offer ingest is idempotent on `offer_uuid`; lifecycle transitions are guarded no-ops. Re-delivery from the daemon/extension is expected and safe by design.
- **Legacy names.** `Ridy` / `DASHCAM` / `fleeteye` in code and docs all mean Reidey. Env `FCM_SERVER_KEY` in the prod compose is legacy and unused by the current HTTP-v1 sender.
- **`status = 'rejected'` now has TWO meanings, and one column tells them apart.** It is either (a) the old, inferred one — the offer reached the driver and the driver did not take it (`DriverStatusIngestor` / `expirePending`) — or (b) **we** declined it on the dispatch panel before the driver ever acted (§2.7). The discriminator is **`dispatch_offers.dispatch_rejected_at`**: non-null = (b). Never read `status` alone for a rate, a badge or a report. Use `DispatchOffer::scopeReachedDriver()` (`whereNull('dispatch_rejected_at')`) as the denominator — a panel-declined offer is excluded from total AND accepted and counted separately as `dispatch_declined`, because it never reached the driver. `OfferLifecycle::takeableGuard()` also starts with that `whereNull`, so a confirmed dispatch decline can never be re-attributed as an accept by a later status edge.
- **The dispatch-decline kill switch is in the DB, not env.** It is `tenants.settings['dispatch_reject']['enabled']` (`Tenant::dispatchRejectEnabled()` / `setDispatchRejectEnabled()`), absent = OFF — **deliberately not** an env var, because the CI deploy **rewrites the production `.env` from GitHub secrets on every push to `main`**: an env flag would be reset by the next unrelated deploy (and a secret that is "only for prod" has bitten this project before — see the DB-password outage). Flip it per company from the **admin companies page** (the switch beside El-Professor; `PATCH /api/v1/admin/companies/{tenant}/dispatch-reject`, super-admin only — a company cannot open it for itself). Turning it ON asks for confirmation because it starts sending real declines to Uber; turning it OFF does not, because it is the kill switch and has to stay one click away. It gates the decline endpoints, the bare instant push, the inline `OfferBroadcast` fields, `can_decline` (so the app's pop-up and its notification button both disappear), and `dispatch_reject_enabled` on the driver's profile. Closing it revokes nothing and leaves already-queued commands to report their real outcome.
- **The two countdowns measure from DIFFERENT clocks, on purpose — do not "unify" them.** `deadlineOf(receivedAt, windowSeconds, generatedAt?)` in `driver-app/src/components/countdown.tsx` prefers Uber's `offer_generated_at` **only when a caller passes it**. The decline pop-up passes it (there the remaining window *is* the decision, so it must be Uber's real clock). The offer **detail screen deliberately does not** — it predates the feature, and switching it would silently shorten every driver's ring by our ingest latency on every tenant, flag or no flag. Adding the third argument there "for consistency" is a behaviour change to a settled screen, not a cleanup.
- **Prod queue/cache = database, not Redis.** Redis + Horizon exist only in the dev `docker-compose.yml`.
